About Me

I am a PhD student in Mechanical Engineering at The University of Akron, working in the Nourhani Laboratory. My research focuses on active matter, microswimmers, self-phoretic particles, and low-Reynolds-number fluid dynamics.

My work combines computational modeling, numerical simulations, high-performance computing, and theoretical analysis. I am particularly interested in understanding how interactions between individual microswimmers produce collective behavior, pattern formation, phase transitions, and spatial organization.

I also study the motion of self-phoretic Janus particles near solid boundaries. This work examines fixed points, periodic trajectories, limit cycles, and the effects of particle geometry and surface properties on near-wall dynamics.

Research Areas

  • Active matter and collective behavior
  • Magnetic and polar microswimmers
  • Self-phoresis of Janus particles
  • Low-Reynolds-number hydrodynamics
  • Boundary element methods
  • Particle-based simulations
  • High-performance scientific computing
